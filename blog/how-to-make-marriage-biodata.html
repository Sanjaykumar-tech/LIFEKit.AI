How to Make a Marriage Biodata That Stands Out

A practical guide — 10 tips that actually make a difference, from formatting to the small details that get you more responses.

📖 6 min read ✍️ LIFEKit Team 📅 January 2025

In India, a marriage biodata is often the first impression you make on a prospective family. It's read in 30 seconds. It's scanned, not studied. And it either gets filed under "let's talk" or "let's move on."

Most biodatas fail for the same reasons: too long, too plain, too generic, or missing key info. Here's how to make one that actually gets responses.

1. Keep it to one page

A biodata is not a resume. It's not a novel. It's a snapshot. If it takes more than 60 seconds to read, you've already lost attention.

One page. One column. Clean sections. Done.

2. Add a good photo — this matters more than you think

Multiple studies across matrimonial platforms show profiles with photos get 10–20× more responses than profiles without them. It's not shallow — it's human.

Choose a clear, well-lit, recent photo. Plain background. Formal or semi-formal. Smile naturally. No group photos. No heavy filters.

A photo takes 30 seconds to add — but it's the single biggest factor in whether your biodata gets a second look.

3. Use clean, readable formatting

Skip Word's default fonts. Skip colored backgrounds. Skip decorative borders that look like MS Paint.

Use one font family, two sizes (headings + body), and plenty of whitespace. The best biodatas look almost boring — that's the point. They're easy to read.

4. Put the most important facts first

Families scan for these in order:

  1. Name + photo (top of the page)
  2. Age / DOB
  3. Height
  4. Education
  5. Occupation
  6. Family background
  7. Contact

5. Be specific, not vague

Vague: "Working in IT."
Specific: "Software Engineer at Infosys, Bangalore, 4 years experience, ₹12 LPA."

Vague: "Good family."
Specific: "Father: Retired bank manager. Mother: Homemaker. 1 elder sister (married)."

Specifics signal seriousness. Vague statements signal someone who didn't care enough to fill it out properly.

6. Mention your education clearly

Degrees matter. Say what you studied, where, and when. If it's a professional degree (B.Tech, MBBS, CA, MBA), highlight it.

7. Get the family section right

This is where most biodatas get messy. Here's what to include:

  • Father's name + occupation
  • Mother's name + occupation
  • Number of siblings + their marital status
  • Family type (nuclear / joint) — optional but useful

8. Add your interests — but be real

"Reading, travelling, music" — everyone writes this. It tells the reader nothing.

Try: "I run a book club on weekends and have trekked the Himalayas twice." Or: "I play the sitar and cook Italian food on Sundays."

9. Choose the right format to share

WhatsApp: Share as PDF, not as a photo of a Word doc. PDFs are crisp on all phones.
Email: PDF attached, with a short 2-line intro in the body.
Matrimonial apps: Keep a digital copy ready — you'll use it multiple times.

10. Proofread before you send

This is the one everyone skips. Typos, wrong dates, mismatched fonts, "Priya" spelled as "Priyaa" in one place and "Prya" in another.

Read it out loud. Send it to a friend. Fix everything. A clean biodata signals someone who cares about details — and that's what families look for in a partner.

The 30-second checklist

  • ✅ One page
  • ✅ Good photo
  • ✅ Clean fonts
  • ✅ Key facts first
  • ✅ Specific details
  • ✅ Family section complete
  • ✅ 2–3 real interests
  • ✅ Shared as PDF
  • ✅ Zero typos
  • ✅ Contact info correct
