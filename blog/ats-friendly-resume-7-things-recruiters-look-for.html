ATS-Friendly Resume: 7 Things Recruiters Look For

Over 75% of resumes are rejected by software before a human ever sees them. Here's how to get past the robots — and impress the humans.

📖 5 min read ✍️ LIFEKit Team 📅 January 2025

You spent hours on your resume. Carefully chose every word. Formatted it beautifully. Sent it out to 20 companies.

And heard nothing.

Here's the harsh truth: your resume was probably never read by a human. It was rejected by an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) before anyone saw it.

What is an ATS?

An ATS is software companies use to filter resumes. When you apply online, your resume goes into the ATS. The system scans it for keywords, structure, and formatting. It then ranks candidates and shows the top ones to a human recruiter.

Up to 75% of resumes are rejected at this stage. Not because the candidate isn't qualified — but because the resume is unreadable by the software.

ATS software doesn't reject you. It just fails to find you — if your resume doesn't match what it's looking for.

How an ATS reads your resume

It doesn't see columns, graphics, or colors. It sees plain text extracted from your PDF or Word file. If your resume was designed with a fancy two-column layout or lots of icons, the ATS might extract garbled nonsense.

That's the entire problem. And that's why these 7 things matter.

1. Keywords that match the job description

This is the single most important thing. ATS software scans for the exact words in the job posting. If the job says "React.js" and your resume says "ReactJS" — it might miss you.

Look at the job description. Highlight the skills, tools, and responsibilities it mentions. Use those exact words in your resume.

💡 Pro tip

If the job says "customer service" and you wrote "client support" — change it. ATS doesn't know they mean the same thing.

2. A simple, single-column layout

Two-column resumes look nice in design tools. But when an ATS reads them, it often mixes the columns up.

Example — your resume looks like this:

  • Left column: Experience
  • Right column: Skills + Education

The ATS might read it as: "Experience Skills Education..." — a garbled mess.

❌ Avoid

Two-column layouts, tables, text boxes, headers/footers, graphics, icons.

✅ Use

One column. Top to bottom. Simple headings. Clean paragraphs and bullet points.

3. The right file format

ATS systems prefer PDF or .docx. Most modern ATS handle PDFs fine. But if the job listing specifically says ".docx only" — send .docx.

Avoid: .pages (Mac only), .jpg (an image resume is unreadable), .txt (too plain).

4. Standard section headings

ATS software recognizes standard section names. Creative headings confuse it.

❌ Avoid

"My Journey", "Where I've Been", "The Skills I Bring", "Things I Love Doing"

✅ Use

"Experience", "Education", "Skills", "Projects", "Certifications", "Summary"

5. Dates in a standard format

Use Month Year – Month Year format consistently. E.g. "Jan 2022 – Dec 2023". Or just years: "2022 – 2023".

Don't write: "2 years at Google" or "Summer of 2022". ATS can't parse those.

6. Contact info in the body (not header/footer)

Many resumes put phone/email in the page header (the very top margin). ATS sometimes skips headers entirely.

Put your contact info in the main body — first few lines of the page. Name, phone, email, LinkedIn.

💡 Pro tip

Use a professional email. Firstname.lastname@gmail.com beats priya_2004_xxx@yahoo.com.

7. No images or graphics

Icons, skill bars, pie charts, company logos — none of these are read by ATS. They can actually make your resume unreadable.

Don't show "JavaScript: 90%" as a progress bar. Just write "JavaScript" in a Skills section.

❌ Avoid

Charts, bars, icons, logos, decorative elements, colored backgrounds.

The ATS-friendly resume formula

Here's what actually works:

  1. Name + title + contact at the top (in body, not header)
  2. Professional summary — 2–3 lines with keywords
  3. Skills — plain text list, matching job description
  4. Experience — company, role, dates, achievements with numbers
  5. Education — degree, college, year
  6. Projects — for freshers, this is critical

One column. Simple fonts. No colors. No graphics. Plain and boring — exactly what ATS loves.

The "achieve with numbers" rule

Once you're past the ATS, a human reads your resume. Humans care about impact.

Weak: "Responsible for managing social media."
Strong: "Grew Instagram following from 2K to 45K in 8 months through content strategy."

Always quantify. Numbers stand out. Vague responsibilities don't.

Test your resume before applying

Free ATS scanners online (like Jobscan, Resumeworded) will tell you if your resume is ATS-friendly. Run it through once before applying.

Also: paste your resume into Notepad. If it looks garbled or out of order, an ATS will read it the same way.
